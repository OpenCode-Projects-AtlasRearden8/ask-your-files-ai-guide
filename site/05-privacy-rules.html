Chapter 5 — Privacy Rules for Personal Files

You're going to paste some of your own documents into AI conversations. Good documents to paste: contracts, manuals, manuscripts, meeting notes, letters that have already been through an appeals process. Documents to keep away: anything you'd be uncomfortable finding, quoted, in someone else's hands.

The principles in this chapter are short, and they're the whole policy:

Rule 1 — Local first. If the tool can run on your computer, run it there. Every tool in this guide does. A local model can't leak what it never sees.

Rule 2 — Minimum sufficient data. Paste the smallest set that can answer the question. Ask "which paragraph do I need?" before "should I paste the file?" — often it's one paragraph, and Chapter 2's search finds it.

Rule 3 — Redact by default. Names, account numbers, member IDs, dates of birth, full addresses, phone numbers: replace with placeholders before pasting. Policy #REDACTED-01, my spouse, my primary clinic. The AI answers your question just as well with placeholders 99% of the time.

Rule 4 — Never paste credentials or money. Passwords, API keys, full payment card numbers, bank logins, one-time codes. If a document contains one, redact the line. This rule is absolute; there's no "just this once."

Rule 5 — Understand the tool before the file. Cloud chat tools vary: some train on your messages, some promise otherwise, some have per-chat settings. When in doubt, redact more, or take the local route. Corporate accounts can have separate logging policies from personal ones — don't mix them.

The redaction habit, made concrete

Keep a tiny scratch file (or just your editor's find-and-replace) with placeholders you reuse, so redaction takes thirty seconds:

OriginalPlaceholder
John AsdaleI / the patient
Bill Henderson (third party)Person A
Account 4471-0092-33Account ###-END-33
DOB 03/14/74DOB [redacted]
123 Maple Streetmy home address

Two tricks that make this sustainable:

  • Keep the tail digits. You often need the fact that two documents share an account, not the number itself. ###-END-33 keeps the match, drops the ID.
  • Redact the copy, never the original. Work from a local copy when a document will be pasted at all. The original files stay intact, exactly as filed.

What "local" actually buys you

Local-first isn't only a privacy virtue; it removes decisions. When the model, the embeddings, and the index all live on your machine:

  • no upload step exists to get wrong,
  • no account, no terms-of-service gray area, no retention setting,
  • the index file (quotes.json) is just a file you can delete, like any other.

The trade-off is real: local models, especially small ones, are less polished than the big cloud models. For quote-reading and summarizing, they're usually plenty — and when they're not, Rule 2 (minimum data, redacted) is your cloud escape hatch, used deliberately.

Personal-data radar (the 60-second version)

Before any paste, scan for: names of other people, dates of birth, account or member numbers, street addresses, phone/email, anything medical-visit-shaped, anything third-party-named, anything you'd be sad to find screenshot in an unexpected place. If you see placeholders in your pasted text and the question still works — pause and confirm — you're in good shape. If the question only works with the private detail visible, that's a sign the question itself might be one for a professional, not a chat window (see Chapter 6).

A note on scanned documents and photos

Scans and photos of documents carry all the same personal data as the paper — and sometimes more (a photo of a bill on your desk may include handwritten notes, or fragments of other mail). Redact or crop images too, and remember that OCR tools (including the ones that would let you use Chapter 3 on a scan) may themselves be cloud services. Prefer local OCR (many local tools exist) when the pages are sensitive.

In one sentence

Run it local, paste the least, redact by habit, and never let credentials or money across the line — pretty soon it's a reflex, not an effort.
